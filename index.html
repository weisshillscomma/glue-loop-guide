One Loop.Zero Bridges.

I'm convinced that the Glue Loop is the greatest 3.35-mile race in the world. It is also one of the hardest 3.35-mile races in the world, if you only count races that are exactly one lap of Prospect Park and start at the Peristyle, of which there is one.

The Glue Loop baits you into surging four separate times: off the start, at the bottom of the East Drive hill, down the West Drive, and then a mad dash to the porta potties after you cross the finish. The consequences for falling for the first three surges seem trivial until roughly minute fourteen, when your legs set like craft glue on a popsicle stick. Every member of Glue Factory has been that popsicle stick at least once. It's practically the initiation. This guide exists so you only have to be it the one time. No guarantees that the fourth surge won't occur every time you run.

Section 03 · Getting to the Start

The race before the race

The Peristyle is in Prospect Park. You are, statistically, already in Brooklyn. Getting a handful of runners there is not its own event, but I am going to treat it like one anyway.

New this year — read this even if you've run it before: nothing has changed. There is no transport selection window. Your bib has no transport assignment printed on it, just a number and the quiet weight of expectation. Showing up by a mode you weren't assigned will result in absolutely nothing.

The train

The B or Q to Parkside Ave, then a short walk into the park. My pick, if I'm being honest about how I feel before 8 a.m.

The bike

Lock it to something near the start. Do not lock it to another runner.

The warm-up jog

Run there. It counts as a warm-up and gives you something to complain about at the start line.

Work backwards from the "go"

  • −60 minWake up. Outfit was laid out the night before, so your brain makes zero decisions.
  • −50 minCoffee. A banana. Not the second banana.
  • −30 minLeave. Check the group chat for the start time, which has moved.
  • −20 minArrive at the Peristyle. Pick up your bib, pin it on (four pins, not two), and add your stuff to the pile.
  • −15 minEasy jog, a few strides, small talk you will not remember.
  • −2 minSomeone asks "are we doing this?" We are doing this.
  • 0:00No cannon. No Sinatra. Someone says "go," probably.
Section 04 · The Weather

It will be outside

The Glue Loop is held in a park, which places it, historically, outdoors. Conditions range from "lovely" to "why did we pick today." Check the forecast the night before; dress for the run, not the standing around, because there is barely any standing around.

Cold · under 45°F

Long sleeves, gloves you can tuck in your shorts by the top of the hill. You'll be warm by the zoo.

Ideal · 45–60°F

Singlet and shorts. The biggest mistake I see is a jacket at the start line of a 3-mile race.

Warm · over 60°F

Slow the goal a bit from the start. The East Drive hill gets full sun and zero sympathy.

The wind rule: with no bridges, the wind is never dangerous, only rude. Into a headwind on the West Drive, hold effort and let pace go. The cyclist rule: stay in the runner lane. They are not racing you. Some of them think they are.

Section 05 · Race Day

The details that trip people up

Waves

Wave 1When the club says go
Wave 2Whoever was late

You may drop back to Wave 2. You cannot move up, because Wave 1 is gone.

Bag check

The Peristyle. Everyone leaves their things there while they race, and it works on the honor system and the fact that nobody wants your sweaty hoodie. Make your bag recognizable; you'll be finding it with your brain at 30% capacity.

Bib pickup

Race morning, at the Peristyle. No expo, no Javits, no ID check. Get there early enough to pin it on straight; a crooked bib is visible in every photo forever.

For your crew

The top of the hill: where a familiar face is worth thirty seconds. The bandshell stretch: easy to reach, and you'll be fast there. The finish: mostly so someone can take the photo.

Your watch

GPS works fine in the park. Use it. That said, I'll still pretend there's a hard-won wisdom to pressing lap at every mile, because that's the kind of guide this is.

Security, in one line

Watch out for the dog on the extendable leash. There is always one.

The shirt

Every finisher gets one. It says something to the effect of "Glue Loop · Prospect Park · 1 Loop," which is both a souvenir and the entire course description. It is, by any reasonable measure, the most desirable garment in Brooklyn running. People have run the Glue Loop for the shirt alone. People have been seen wearing the shirt to other people's races. This is encouraged.

Size up if you plan on wearing it over layers in November. Size down if you plan on being photographed in it.
